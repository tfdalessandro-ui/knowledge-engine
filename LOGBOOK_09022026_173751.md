## Judgment-set design decisions

- No pre-existing document corpus was available to hand-label against (the
  target folder was empty), and the prompt explicitly allows "whatever local
  documents are convenient" rather than waiting for a real corpus. Wrote a
  25-document synthetic seed corpus (`scripts/seed_corpus.py` →
  `data/corpus/*.txt`) covering the knowledge-engine domain itself (BM25,
  nDCG, hybrid search, CPU inference, Ubuntu/Hetzner facts, etc.) — informative
  short paragraphs, not filler, so relevance judgments would be meaningful
  rather than arbitrary.
- 63 (query, doc_id, relevance 0-3) triples across 20 queries — within the
  50-100 range asked for, and enforced going forward by
  `test_judgment_set_size` in `tests/eval/test_harness.py` so the set can't
  silently balloon or shrink out of range.
- Included explicit `relevance: 0` rows (near-miss docs a query might
  plausibly retrieve but shouldn't rank) alongside graded 1-3 rows, not just
  positive judgments — closer to how a real judgment set gets built, and it
  gives `recall@20`/`nDCG@10` something to actually discriminate against.

This is the expected shape: `perfect` (judged-relevant docs ranked first) hits
the ceiling on all three metrics by construction; `null` (deterministic
decoy IDs, nothing judged) floors at exactly zero; `shuffled` (fixed
query-agnostic order) lands strictly in between — confirming the harness
actually discriminates between rankings rather than returning a constant.
`shuffled`'s recall@20 (0.758) being much higher than its nDCG@10 (0.193) is
expected too: with a 25-document corpus and k=20, a fixed order finds most
judged docs *somewhere* in the top 20 (decent recall) but rarely puts them
near the top (poor nDCG) — the two metrics are measuring different things on
purpose.
